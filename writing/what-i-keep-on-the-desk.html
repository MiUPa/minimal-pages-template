What I Keep on the Desk

Desks tend to accumulate explanations. Every object promises to improve attention, fix a habit, or represent a better version of yourself. Most of them end up becoming visual debt.

Mine has become simpler over time. The objects that remain are not there because they are aesthetically correct. They remain because I still use them without needing to think about it.

A notebook for fragments

I keep a small notebook open near the keyboard. It is not for polished plans. It holds rough sentences, temporary checklists, and diagrams that are easier to draw than to keep in working memory.

One good light

Better light quietly improves everything on the desk. It reduces the fatigue that makes you blame your tools for your own bad posture and late hours.

A short list of defaults

I also keep a card with a few reminders:

  • Close what you are not using.
  • Write the next step down before you stop.
  • Prefer one good tool over three interesting ones.

These are small things, but they reduce the cost of restarting. That matters more than having a desk that photographs well.
